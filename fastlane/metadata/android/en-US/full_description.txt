Is my cluster okay, and if not, what exactly is wrong?

Kubernanny answers that one question for self-hosted Kubernetes. A small agent in your cluster recognizes what you run (Longhorn, Argo CD, cert-manager, CloudNativePG, Prometheus, Loki, kured and about 70 more), checks each of them the way you would by hand, and shows each cluster's traffic light in the nanny's eye. Below it: only what is broken right now, why, and what usually fixes it.

Made for the moment your phone buzzes at 4:10 in the morning. Not another cluster browser, but a clear answer.

WHAT IT CHECKS
• Nodes: NotReady, pressure, RAM and disk. Planned kured reboots are a warning, not an alarm.
• etcd: members, quorum, snapshots
• Workloads: CrashLoopBackOff with the logs of the crashed run, stuck rollouts, failed CronJobs
• Volumes: pending, lost, nearly full
• The standard alerts of the community rule sets, built in: latency between etcd members, fsync and commit durations, filesystems and volumes about to fill up, expiring certificates, DNS errors, and whether the reserved resources still fit when the largest node fails. Most of them need Prometheus.

DETAILS WHEN YOU LOOK CLOSER
• CPU and RAM per node and per workload: used, reserved (requests) and limit
• Restarts with their reason, such as OOMKilled at the memory limit
• Every service in detail, such as CloudNativePG clusters with primary and backups, Longhorn volumes with their replicas, Argo CD apps, certificates, Prometheus targets, Alertmanager alerts and silences
• Ready to sync: Argo CD apps with manual sync and a change in Git, with the revision, images and resources that change
• Backups of CloudNativePG, Longhorn, etcd and Velero at a glance, including what has none
• The last seven days: every incident with start and duration, plus history for CPU, RAM, network and etcd
• Widgets with each cluster's traffic light

ACTIONS, SAFELY
Four, and only on an open problem or an app that is ready to sync: sync and hard refresh an Argo CD app, restart a workload, start a CloudNativePG backup. The agent takes the target from its own check, never from the app. The app cannot delete anything. Rolling back Argo CD apps is there only if you switch it on. The agent can be made strictly read-only.

NOTIFICATIONS
The agent reports problems itself once they have lasted five minutes, again when they turn critical, and when they are resolved:
• as a push straight to the app, end-to-end encrypted: neither the relay nor Google or Apple can read it. If you like, every real push starts with a sign only your device knows. This needs the agent to use the optional relay, the public one or your own.
• or through ntfy, Discord, Slack, Gotify or a webhook.
Known problems can be silenced for up to a week, and a maintenance window holds notifications back.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• End-to-end encrypted with Noise and your device's key. Ingress, proxies, the relay and Wi-Fi only see ciphertext, which is why plain HTTP in your LAN or VPN is fine.
• No account, no tracking, no ads. Without the relay and push, the app talks only to your agent. With them, sealed messages travel through the relay and Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging.
• Your device's private key never leaves it. A lost phone is revoked with one tap.

GETTING STARTED
1. Install the agent with the Helm chart or kubectl apply.
2. Scan the QR code in the agent's log with your camera. Done.

No cluster at hand? The built-in demo shows three invented clusters, one per traffic light color.

Open source (Apache-2.0) and free, with no subscription and no pro version: github.com/kubernanny/kubernanny
